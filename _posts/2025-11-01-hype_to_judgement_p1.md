# Test Automation in the Age of AI — From Hype to Judgment

What 18+ years in QA taught me about where AI helps — and where it quietly hurts?

Why I’m writing about AI and Test Automation now ?

I’ve been in test automation long enough to see multiple waves come and go.
Record & playback.
 Framework-heavy automation.
 CI/CD pipelines.
 Shift-left testing.
 And now — AI everywhere.

Each wave promised to “fix testing.”
And each wave taught me something different.

## What I’ve learned is this:
 Tools change fast, but the fundamentals don’t.

I’m not anti-AI. In fact, I actively use it.

But I am wary of shallow adoption — where speed, demos, and buzzwords replace thinking, design, and judgment.

Today, I see many teams combining a toxic speed culture with AI, hoping it will magically compensate for broken test strategies, unclear ownership, and fragile automation.
The result isn’t productivity — it’s burnout, mistrust in tests, and constant firefighting.
One thing that’s often missing in these conversations is a clear distinction between:

## The Test Life Cycle
 <p>
 – Understanding the system<br>
 – Identifying risks<br>
 – Designing meaningful scenarios<br>
 – Validating behavior and outcomes<br>
 – Learning from failures<br>
</p>

## The Automation Life Cycle
 <p>
 – Deciding what to automate<br>
 – Choosing the right layer (unit, API, UI)<br>
 – Designing for stability and maintainability<br>
 – Managing test data and environments<br>
 – Keeping feedback trustworthy over time<br>
 </p>

AI can help in both — but only if the thinking comes first.

What I want to write about in this series is not “which tool to use” or “how fast we can generate tests.”
 I want to talk about judgment:
<p>- Where AI genuinely adds value<br>
- Where it quietly makes things worse<br>
- And how experienced engineers can use it without losing clarity, quality, or themselves in the process

</p>

Because here’s my honest belief:
AI didn’t break test automation.It exposed weaknesses that were already there.
Over the next few posts, I’ll share real experiences, patterns that worked, mistakes that hurt, and how I now think about AI across the test and automation life cycles — calmly, practically, and without hype.
If you work in testing or engineering:  What excites you about AI right now — and what secretly worries you?I’d genuinely like to hear.
